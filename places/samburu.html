THE NORTHERN FRONTIER

A different landscape, different wildlife.

Samburu National Reserve sits in Kenya’s arid north along the Ewaso Ng’iro River. Riverine forest and greener banks sit against dry, open country, creating a very different feel from the southern savannah parks.

Samburu is known for several northern species often grouped as the “Samburu Special Five”: Grevy’s zebra, reticulated giraffe, gerenuk, beisa oryx and Somali ostrich. These are wild animals, not a checklist; sightings depend on conditions and luck.

WHAT TO SEE

Follow the river country.

Game drives often focus on the contrast between the river corridor and the surrounding dry country. A guide can explain how animals use the area and what is active during your dates.

  • Special Five: ask your guide about the five northern species, while keeping expectations realistic.
  • Birdlife: bring binoculars and ask which river and dry-country habitats to visit.
  • Local context: choose community visits arranged with local hosts and agree on expectations in advance.
TRIP PLANNING

Allow time for the distance.

Samburu is a substantial journey from Nairobi by road. Flights can shorten the transfer, but schedules, baggage limits and camp pickups need to be checked for your dates.

  • Stay: compare camp location, river access and transfer time to the reserve.
  • Comfort: ask what drinking water, shade and midday rest arrangements are provided.
  • Combine: Laikipia can be a logical next stop; request a route with actual drive or flight times.

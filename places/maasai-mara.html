THE RESERVE

A classic safari, with room to explore.

The Maasai Mara is a large grassland reserve in south-western Kenya, named for the Maasai people and the Mara River. Kenya’s tourism board highlights its Big Five wildlife and the seasonal Great Migration. Animal movements vary from year to year, so no sighting or crossing can be promised.

Three or more nights gives you time for several game drives without rushing between parks. When comparing stays, ask whether the camp is inside the reserve or in a neighbouring conservancy; rules, access and fees can differ.

WHAT TO DO

Time in the reserve.

Most visits are built around guided game drives. Depending on the operator, location and conditions, you can ask about a full-day drive, a hot-air balloon flight, or a community-led cultural visit.

  • Game drives: morning and afternoon drives, or ask whether a full day with a picnic lunch is possible.
  • Balloon flights: optional, weather-dependent and usually priced separately; confirm pickup, landing and transfer arrangements.
  • Community visits: ask who runs the experience and how the visit is arranged with local hosts.
TRIP PLANNING

Choose your base carefully.

The Mara is a long way from Nairobi by road. A flight can reduce transfer time, but adds cost and luggage limits. Your camp’s location also shapes how early you leave and how long you spend travelling to viewing areas.

  • First safari: stay in one area for several nights rather than changing camp every day.
  • Season: busy periods and migration timing vary; ask what conditions are expected for your dates.
  • Quote: request an itemised price showing accommodation, meals, transfers, fees and exclusions.
